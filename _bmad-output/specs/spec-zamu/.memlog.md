- (capability) CAP-4 [Must] Public queue: anyone can see ranked order by score and each parent can see their place and expected turn
- (capability) CAP-5 [Must] Case records immutable: evidence + GPS locked at capture; any change creates a new linked case, original preserved and visible

- (capability) CAP-7 [Should] School confirms fees received, closing the loop (simulated in demo)

- (capability) CAP-9 [Should] Anonymous USSD report (skipped turn, amount mismatch, ineligible beneficiary) opens an independent review case
- (capability) CAP-10 [Should] Role-based access and defined stage flow; only permitted role can advance a stage
- (capability) CAP-11 [Should] Admin dashboard to run a round: open window, review cases, advance stages, see queue and totals

- (decision) Scoring model (resolves Q3): points table, max 100, higher = greater need. Fee balance 35 and house type 30 (highest); livestock 20 and land 15 (medium). Reason text names top 2 contributors
- (assumption) Inputs user did not name (economic activity, orphan/single-parent, siblings in school, disability, school level) excluded from PoC score — pitch as configurable future inputs
- (decision) Turn ordering (resolves Q5): option B — queue priority = need score + 10 per consecutive round applied without award; bonus shown separately from need score
- (assumption) Waiting bonus resets to 0 when child receives an award; no cap on bonus; ties broken by earlier application time
- (decision) Queue privacy (resolves Q2): PUBLIC = position, need score, waiting bonus, award amount, school, masked name (initial + partial surname) + case ID. PARENT-ONLY via USSD = own full score breakdown. REVIEWER-ONLY = evidence photos, GPS, parent phone
- (decision) Queue surface (resolves Q4): read-only public web queue page per round (mobile-friendly, stands in for list at school) PLUS parent USSD position lookup; public page shows only the PUBLIC fields
- (decision) Data protection (resolves Q1): real USSD consent screen before apply (decline = no application); by design: masked public names, reviewer-only evidence, anonymous reports, synthetic data; pitch: DPIA + ODPC registration before any pilot
- (decision) Retention: case records (awards, scores, evidence) kept minimum 7 years per user (public-funds audit trail; fits immutability + Auditor General mandate), then deleted
- (question) Cite the specific statute behind the 7-year minimum in the written summary — Kenya DPA 2019 itself only says 'no longer than necessary'; the minimum likely comes from public finance/audit record rules
- (note) Research Q6: NG-CDF = national government fund of >=2.5% of national share of revenue (NG-CDF Act s.4), allocated to constituencies under Constitution Art 206(2)(c); Board releases to constituency accounts quarterly 25% tranches (s.39); bursaries count as development projects capped at 35% of constituency allocation (s.48) unless Board approves more (s.48A); monthly receipts/disbursement records (s.38-39); audited by Auditor-General (s.11)
- (note) Research Q6: county/ward bursaries run under county-specific bursary fund Acts from county budgets (County Revenue Fund); Auditor-General audits them (e.g. Kwale County Bursary Fund 2021). Controller of Budget authorises withdrawals from public funds for both routes (Constitution Art 228)
- (note) Evidence for pitch: Auditor-General 2023 NG-CDF reports — Lang'ata KES 52.9M bursaries with 'no clear criteria' and no acknowledgement letters; Sirisia KES 35.6M unverifiable; Kapseret KES 44.7M unverified; Saku KES 9M without student lists. FY2023/24: 7 counties (incl. Nairobi, Garissa, Isiolo) unable to account for >KES 880M bursary funds; CoB reports bursary accounting officers failing to file quarterly reports
- (decision) Mandate claim (resolves Q6) reframed: leverage is not a single switch but two existing oversight chokepoints both routes share — CoB withdrawal approval and Auditor-General audit. Pitch: Zamu produces the beneficiary list, criteria, and school acknowledgements auditors currently find missing. NG-CDF Board can also set bursary procedures nationally for all 290 constituencies
- (note) Research retention: no single statute found setting a 7-year minimum for public-entity records. 7 years applies under AML regulations (POCAMLA) and is the common corporate accounting norm; procurement records 6 yrs (PPADA), tax records 5 yrs (Tax Procedures Act); Data Protection Act 2019 requires 'no longer than necessary'
- (decision) Retention framing: keep case records minimum 7 years as an audit-trail design choice aligned to Kenya's longest financial record norms — do not cite a statute as requiring it; deletion of public records would also need national archives approval (flag, verify)
