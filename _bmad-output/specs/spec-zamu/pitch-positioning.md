# Pitch positioning — Zamu

Load-bearing for judged criteria (uniqueness, scalability, presentation/track alignment).

## Uniqueness vs HELB

HELB already proves the model in Kenya: means-tested application, status updates, payment direct to the institution. Zamu applies it to the most politicised, least digitised layer — ward, constituency, and county bursaries — and adds what HELB lacks: a **public turn-queue** and **witnessed, immutable evidence**. The queue is the core: it breaks deliberate opacity and removes the scarcity-driven reason to seek favours.

## Trust model

Many independent witnesses, no single point to bribe: parents (queue + SMS), school (fee confirmation), CHVs/teachers (evidence), public totals. Attacks named and addressed: bribing a CHV (multiple witnesses, AI photo check as Could), hiding assets before a visit (witnessed geotagged evidence), altering records at the office (CAP-5).

## Scalability

- **Fixed integrity core:** immutable records, evidence verification, tokenization, core data management.
- **Configurable per country:** language, currency, funding cycle, funding bodies, evidence capturers.
- Problem is universal (opaque allocation of public support); structures (chiefs, CDF, MCAs) are local configuration.

## Adoption without officials

Local officials lose patronage, so adoption comes through oversight bodies that already control both funding routes:

- **NG-CDF** (constituency): national fund of ≥2.5% of national government revenue (NG-CDF Act s.4); Board releases quarterly tranches to constituencies (s.39); bursaries capped at 35% of constituency allocation (s.48) unless the Board approves more (s.48A); audited by the Auditor-General (s.11). The **NG-CDF Board** can set one bursary procedure for every constituency.
- **County/ward bursaries**: county-specific bursary fund Acts, funded from county budgets; audited by the Auditor-General.
- **Shared chokepoints**: the Controller of Budget authorises withdrawals for both (Constitution Art. 228); the Auditor-General audits both.

Pitch: Zamu generates exactly what auditors report missing — criteria, beneficiary lists, school acknowledgements. "No clean audit, no next allocation."

## Evidence for the problem slide

- Auditor-General, 2023 NG-CDF audits: Lang'ata KES 52.9M in bursaries with "no clear criteria" and no acknowledgement letters; Sirisia KES 35.6M unverifiable; Kapseret KES 44.7M unverified; Saku KES 9M paid without student lists.
- Auditor-General, FY2023/24: seven counties (incl. Nairobi, Garissa, Isiolo) unable to account for over KES 880M in bursary funds.
- Controller of Budget: bursary fund accounting officers failing to submit quarterly reports.

Sources: NG-CDF Act No. 30 of 2015 (rev. 2022) — ngcdf.go.ke; Eastleigh Voice (NG-CDF audit, county audit); Education News; Daily Nation.

## Framing

Zamu cannot grow the pot; it makes waiting fair and predictable. One line: *public money made visible, so bursaries are a right, not a favour.* Name: *Zamu* — Swahili for "turn".
