- (decision) Facilitator mode; techniques: First Principles, Five Whys, Disney Method
- (technique) started First Principles Thinking
- (insight) Core pain: public doesn't know about government initiatives meant for them — e.g. CDF bursaries, immunization drives, local government projects
- (insight) Info chain today: chief -> elders -> word of mouth -> needy parent; parents only learn because they're already hunting for money
- (insight) Result: parents queue at chief's camp from 3am on distribution day — info arrives as a rumor of a date/place, not details (eligibility, amount, docs, criteria)
- (insight) Parents apply to several bursary sources at once (MCA ward fund, MP/NG-CDF, governor/county) — it's a gamble: may get all, some, or none
- (insight) Application is blind: no visibility into criteria, status, or chances after submitting forms
- (insight) Outcome is only visible later as a list pinned at the chief's office (who got it, how much)
- (insight) Corruption: people with connections in those offices get priority — allocation isn't merit/need-based and parents can't see or contest it
- (idea) Bedrock truth: parent must be able to SEE the selection criteria
- (idea) Bedrock truth: parent must be able to SEE which stage the selection process is at (status tracking)
- (idea) Bedrock truth: parent must be able to SEE how the money is distributed (who got what, how much)
- (idea) Beneficiaries confirm they actually received the money (recipient-side confirmation closes the loop)
- (idea) Total bursary budget is public and every shilling of distribution is visible — allocations must add up to the budget
- (idea) Each applicant gets a suitability score (not just 'needy' score) showing why they were/weren't selected; score shown across the whole distribution list
- (idea) Suitability score must be computed from verifiable data, not officials' discretion
- (idea) Hard constraint: neediest parents have NO digital footprint — no bank statements, some lack national ID cards
- (idea) Only reliable digital record: the child's school records (enrolment, fees, performance)
- (idea) Verification must come from visible, physical reality: visual confirmation of livelihood — house, livestock (cows, goats), land size, title deed
- (idea) Economic activity (what the family does for a living) as a verifiable scoring input
- (idea) Evidence capturers: community health volunteers (CHVs) — already visit households
- (idea) Evidence capturers: teachers — already know the child and family
- (idea) Geotagged photos of the household/assets as tamper-resistant livelihood evidence
- (question) Attack: bribe the CHV doing the verification
- (question) Attack: hide assets (move cows to a neighbor's) before the visit
- (question) Attack: alter/change records after submission, at the office during approval
- (idea) Defined process flow with role-based access — prevents unauthorized use/editing at each stage
- (idea) Photos + GPS locked to the specific case at capture; immutable (append-only)
- (idea) Any correction = new case/amendment raised, original preserved — alterations leave a visible trail
- (idea) Public accountability: selection list (with scores) posted publicly at the school
- (idea) SMS alerts to all CHVs and teachers when lists/decisions change — works on basic phones, creates many outside watchers
- (technique) started Five Whys
- (insight) Why 1: officials favor connected families to build personal debts/loyalty; elected officials (MCA/MP/governor) use bursaries as patronage to win votes next election
- (insight) Why 2: official personalises public money as a gift from himself/his office and takes the credit
- (insight) Why 3: parents don't know bursary money comes from public taxes/budget — they see it as a favour, not a right
- (insight) Why 4: budgets ARE published — but as online PDFs nobody reads; information exists but is unreachable for village parents
- (insight) Why 5 / ROOT CAUSE: officials benefit when parents stay uninformed — opacity is deliberate, so nobody inside the system will fix it; the push must come from outside/below
- (insight) Why 1 (demand side): stiff competition for scarce bursaries -> parents willingly seek favours to get priority -> this rewards officials for trading bursaries for political loyalty (two-sided corruption loop)
- (insight) Informal rotation rule: bursaries given 'in turns' — get it this term, likely skipped next; parents who need continuous support (every term/year) use favours to stay on the list consistently. Need is continuous but allocation is rotational
- (insight) Branch-2 root: scarcity — too little bursary money spread across too many families, so rotation + favour-seeking follow. App can't grow the budget; it can only make allocation fair and predictable
- (idea) Transparent public queue: each parent can see their place in the bursary queue and when their turn is due — predictability removes the incentive to buy priority with favours
- (technique) started Disney Method (Dreamer room)
- (idea) Dreamer: proactive SMS in the parent's language (Swahili) announcing bursary window is open
- (idea) Dreamer: SMS tells the parent it's HER TURN to apply and how much money is available this round
- (idea) Dreamer: no repeat paperwork — student records already on file; returning applicants apply in one step
- (idea) Dreamer: new child = one-time in-person verification at the office, then on file for future rounds
- (idea) Dreamer: apply via USSD code (feature phone, no internet) — dial, press 'apply', then track verification and approval
- (idea) Dreamer: SMS status update at every stage (applied, verified, approved, disbursed)
- (idea) Dreamer: money goes to the school; school confirms fees received — independent end-of-chain confirmation (closes the loop without cash in parent's hands)
- (idea) Dreamer: final SMS reports round totals — how much was distributed and to how many recipients — every parent sees the aggregate
- (idea) Dreamer: anonymous reporting via USSD (skipped turn, amount mismatch, ineligible beneficiary) triggers an independent review — safe to speak up, no retaliation on future turns
